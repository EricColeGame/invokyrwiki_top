## What Is Invokyr? The Co-Op Horror Board Game Explained

Invokyr is a first-person psychological horror game built around a cursed board game. Developer Ludogram — publishing alongside Shochiku — frames it as a co-op experience where the board itself is the antagonist's instrument. You roll, the rules shift, monsters get summoned, and your group tries to reach the end of the board before the Gamemaster finishes you off.

The tone isn't pure dread. Steam's user tags mix survival horror and psychological horror with dark comedy and mystery, which fits a premise this knowingly strange: a forgotten toy that grew cruel. The house is a labyrinth of rooms, and leaving the board to scavenge for dice is often how runs fall apart.

A free demo is already available, so you can test the loop with your group before committing. You can check the full feature list and demo download on [Invokyr's official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3883570/Invokyr/).

## How a Co-Op Run Works: Dice, Turns, and the Gamemaster

The core loop is deceptively simple: play the board, survive what it summons, and reach the end. In practice, three systems keep a session tense.

**The board sets the pace.** Turns trigger events. The developer describes more than 20 events and 15-plus monsters in the Early Access build, plus "increasingly dangerous combinations of rules" as you push toward the ultimate Invokyr Board.

**Dice are a finite resource.** Scattered through the house are special dice, and according to the store description, each can only be used once. That single line changes how a team plays. You aren't managing a cooldown — you're spending a resource that will never come back.

**The house is a hazard, not a safe zone.** Rooms form a labyrinth, and getting lost means you may not make it back to the board in time. Since dice are hidden in the house, someone always has to leave the table.

| System | What it does | Why it matters in co-op |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board turns | Advance play and trigger events | Shared progress means shared blame |
| Events | Random chaos each turn | No two runs unfold the same way |
| Monsters | Unique hunters with their own methods | Forces role splitting |
| Special dice | Single-use powers | Creates scarcity and tough calls |
| Biomes | Themed house zones | Changes what you prepare for |
| Shop and upgrades | Between-board progression | Long-term goals beyond one run |

### Death isn't always the end

One detail worth highlighting for teams: the developers explicitly note that "death is not always the end." That's a meaningful design choice for co-op, because it suggests eliminated players aren't necessarily benched for the rest of the night. Treat it as a mechanic to discover rather than a rule to rely on — but it does soften the sting of a bad roll.

## Team Roles and Dice Strategy for Invokyr Co-Op

Invokyr doesn't advertise rigid classes, so think of roles as coordination habits rather than locked loadouts. Groups that communicate well tend to split duties along these lines.

| Role | Job on the team | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scout | Leave the board, map rooms, locate hidden dice | Getting lost is the biggest risk |
| Anchor | Stay near the board to keep progress moving | Can't help when a monster corners a scout |
| Support | Hold healing dice for emergencies | Wasting a heal early is brutal |
| Disruptor | Use hindering dice to slow hunters | Timing matters more than power |
| Caller | Tracks rules, events, and turn order | Needs everyone listening |

Some general dice strategy that applies to any group size:

- **Don't spend a die because you have it.** Single-use dice are the closest thing to a win condition you control. Hold healing dice until someone is genuinely in danger.
- **Assign dice owners out loud.** In a six-player lobby, ambiguity is how good dice get wasted.
- **Bank hindering dice for the chase, not the encounter.** Slowing a hunter during a scramble usually buys more than a small early advantage.
- **Track which dice you've already burned.** "Choose wisely" is the developer's own warning for a reason.

### Communication beats reflexes

Accessibility notes confirm the game is playable without quick-time events, which tells you something about its design philosophy: Invokyr rewards planning and information-sharing over twitch skill. If your group has one player who naturally narrates what's happening, make that person the caller. In a first-person horror game with random rules, a calm voice on comms is worth more than a fast trigger finger.

## Practical Tips for Your First Invokyr Co-Op Night

A few things to sort out before you press play:

1. **Confirm everyone's specs.** System requirements are still TBA, so nobody should assume their rig is fine.
2. **Run the demo first.** It's free and it's the fastest way to learn whether your group enjoys the loop.
3. **Cap your lobby at a size you can actually coordinate.** Six is the max, not a requirement. Four focused players often beat six scattered ones.
4. **Agree on a dice policy in advance.** Who holds heals, who holds hindrances, who decides when to spend.
5. **Set expectations for Early Access.** The game is a work in progress by design, and content will shift.
6. **Check language support.** English has full audio, and interface and subtitles are available in a dozen additional languages including French, German, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Ukrainian, and Italian.

One more accessibility note for groups with mixed needs: the game supports adjustable text size, subtitle options, custom volume controls, stereo sound, and a no-QTE play style.

## FAQ

### How many players can join an Invokyr co-op session?

Up to six total, including you. The Steam store description phrases it as playing "with up to 5 friends," while the developer's Early Access notes describe a full multiplayer loop for 1 to 6 players. Single-player is also supported.

### Is Invokyr co-op online only?

Yes, based on official listings. The Steam feature set lists single-player, online co-op, and Family Sharing — there's no mention of local or split-screen play. Each player needs their own copy.

### Do you need a full team to enjoy Invokyr co-op?

No. The design supports anywhere from one to six players, and content scales around a shared loop rather than fixed roles. Smaller groups simply have fewer hands to scavenge dice and fewer voices tracking the rules.

### When does Invokyr come out?

Early Access begins October 8, 2026, with a planned window of roughly one year before the 1.0 release. A free demo is available now, and pricing is expected to rise gradually as new content ships — so early adopters pay less, but they also play an unfinished game.

The Gamemaster controls the board. Your group controls how well you communicate, how carefully you spend dice, and whether anyone gets left behind. That's the whole gamble of Invokyr co-op — and it's a good one.
