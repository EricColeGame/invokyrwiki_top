## Co-op Rules That Decide Whether Your Squad Survives

Cross-platform questions get the headlines, but the mechanics inside a lobby decide who makes it out of the estate. Three systems matter most, and all three reward communication over individual heroics.

### Single-Use Dice Are a Shared Economy

Dice are consumable pickups, not infinite rolls. Each recovered die can be used once at the central board table, which turns scavenging into a genuine resource decision rather than a formality.

| Dice Category | Typical Purpose | Allocation Advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard dice | Advance game pieces at the board | Spend steadily, but never while teammates are isolated |
| Utility dice | Healing, hiding, or hindering creatures | Hold for emergencies and assign a dedicated carrier |
| Unknown pickups | Effects unclear until triggered | Test only when the squad is grouped and safe |

Player experience consistently flags wasted utility dice as a top cause of failed runs. A healing die burned during a quiet corridor sweep is a healing die unavailable when a summoned entity blocks the only exit.

### The "Roll Call" Failsafe

Every roll at the central table advances the game pieces — and prompts the Gamemaster to spawn fresh hazards, traps, or hunters somewhere across the estate. Community reports describe the danger landing hardest on whoever is farthest from the board.

| Situation | Risk | Countermeasure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rolling while a teammate searches a distant wing | Sudden isolation, locked doors, ambush | Enforce Roll Call before touching the board |
| Splitting into pairs | Two fronts to defend | Regroup before any roll |
| Ignoring a downed teammate | Loss of a body and carried dice | Weigh rescue against the dice they hold |

The Roll Call rule is simple: no board interaction until every explorer has returned to the central chamber or confirmed a clear retreat path.

### Death Is Not Always the End

Falling in the estate does not necessarily eject you from the session. Downed teammates can be revived, which turns every rescue into a risk calculation. Send someone back and you may lose two players instead of one; leave them and you lose the dice they were carrying.

Solo play is allowed, but the design clearly assumes a squad. Without teammates to draw pursuers away, exploring and puzzle-solving happen under constant pressure — which is exactly the scenario the Roll Call rule exists to prevent in co-op.
