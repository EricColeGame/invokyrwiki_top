## Invokyr 4 Player Lobby Setup: Before You Roll

Setup is not glamorous, but it is where runs are quietly lost. Online co-op means connection quality, patch versions, and role clarity all matter before anyone touches the board.

| Step | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm everyone is on the same build | Early Access patches can land quickly after launch |
| 2 | Test voice chat before the board wakes up | Callouts are your primary defense in a first-person house |
| 3 | Pick the host with the most stable connection | Online co-op lives and dies on latency |
| 4 | Agree on a "no hero plays" rule | Death is not always the end, but it still costs momentum |
| 5 | Assign roles out loud before the first roll | Prevents two players duplicating one job |

A few practical notes: Invokyr lists Online Co-Op, Single-player, and Family Sharing as features, and Steam flags in-game chat and online interactivity as interactive elements. There is no mention of local split-screen, so plan for online play.

## Best Role Setup for an Invokyr 4 Player Team

Four is enough to cover the core jobs without leaving anyone idle. The trick is to divide by function, not by friendship — your closest friend may be a terrible board anchor.

| Role | Core job | Best paired with | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board Anchor | Stays near the board, tracks active rules, calls turns | Support | Getting bored and wandering off |
| Scout | Maps rooms and marks the route back | Dice Runner | Getting lost in the labyrinth |
| Dice Runner | Recovers hidden dice, which are single-use | Scout | Burning a die the second it is found |
| Support / Flex | Handles healing and concealment dice, covers whoever is in trouble | Anyone | Hoarding dice "for later" |

### Pair Up Instead of Splitting Four Ways

The house is a labyrinth of rooms, and the game explicitly warns that losing your way can mean never making it back to the board. Send two pairs rather than four solo explorers. Each pair covers a direction, keeps a verbal thread open, and can drag a downed teammate toward safety. When the Gamemaster's rules shift mid-run — and they will, since the rule system is designed to make every game unfold differently — pairs can regroup faster than scattered individuals.

## Dice, Curses, and Chaos: Strategy for Four Players

Dice are the engine of Invokyr. They are hidden throughout the house, each one can only be used once, and the developer notes that special dice can heal, hide, or hinder those hunting you — but nothing comes without risk. That single sentence should shape every decision your team makes.

| Dice type (as described by the developers) | What it does | Who should carry it | When to spend it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing | Restores a teammate | Support / Flex | After a chase ends, not during one |
| Concealment | Breaks a pursuit | Scout or Dice Runner | The moment a creature commits to you |
| Hinder | Disrupts or slows hunters | Board Anchor | When the team needs a window to reposition |
| Unidentified / risky | Unpredictable effects | Whoever is most expendable | Ideally never on a full-health team |

Three rules of thumb for a four-player squad:

- **Never spend a die to save five seconds.** Single-use means single-use. A die burned on convenience is a die missing from the escape attempt.
- **Protect the board.** It is your home base. A team that loses the board loses its reference point for every rule in play.
- **Expect escalation.** Surviving earns rewards and unlocks more formidable challenges, so the difficulty curve climbs as your group improves.

Also prepare for the house to change character. The developers describe distinct biomes, including a jungle, an ice mountain, and an oversized toy chest. Each one resets your mental map, which is exactly why the Scout role exists.

## Common Four-Player Mistakes to Avoid

- **Splitting four ways in a labyrinth.** Coverage feels efficient until someone cannot find their way back.
- **Hoarding single-use dice.** Saved dice do not carry between runs; use them when they change an outcome.
- **Ignoring rule changes.** The rule system is built to vary each session, so the strategy that worked last run may be a liability now.
- **Playing on mismatched builds.** Early Access means frequent updates. Confirm versions before you invite friends.
- **Giving up on a downed teammate.** Death is not always the end in Invokyr, so a rescue is often still on the table.
