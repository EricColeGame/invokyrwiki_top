## What Is Invokyr Multiplayer, and Why Should You Care?

Invokyr multiplayer turns a dusty 1970s board game into a six-player nightmare, and it arrives on Steam in Early Access on October 8, 2026. If you have ever wanted a horror night where your friends are both your lifeline and your biggest liability, this is the one to watch. Invokyr multiplayer is not a solo campaign with drop-in guests bolted on — it is a cooperative psychological horror experience built from the ground up around a table, a handful of dice, and a Gamemaster who wants you to keep playing.

The premise is simple and nasty. You and your friends are trapped in an abandoned house with a cursed board game humming on the table. Roll the dice, survive whatever gets summoned, and reach the end of the board to win your freedom. Death is not always the end, which means a downed teammate can still matter — and a bad decision by one player can drag the whole group into the dark.

Here is everything worth knowing before you gather your squad.

## How Invokyr Co-Op Sessions Actually Work

Invokyr multiplayer is online co-op. The Steam feature list highlights online co-op and family sharing, along with in-game chat, so the intended flow is a hosted session with voice or text communication rather than a couch split-screen night. If you are planning a group, plan around headsets.

Sessions are structured around the board itself. The group explores the house to recover dice hidden in the rooms, returns to the board to roll, and then deals with whatever the roll summons. Every die can only be used once, which creates the central multiplayer tension: who gets the good roll, and who gets sacrificed for it?

### The Multiplayer Loop, Phase by Phase

| Phase | What the group does | Multiplayer pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore | Scour the labyrinth of rooms for hidden dice and items | Splitting up covers more ground but leaves players isolated |
| Roll | Return to the board and commit a die to the game | A single roll can summon monsters, traps, or curses |
| Survive | Outlast whatever the Gamemaster throws at you | Revives, hiding, and healing dice become group resources |
| Progress | Reach the end of the board and advance | Rules stack up and get more dangerous as you go |

The house itself is not a single repeated hallway. Invokyr includes distinct biomes such as a jungle, an ice mountain, and an oversized toy chest, and getting lost is a real threat. Lose your way in the labyrinth and you may never make it back to the board — which in a co-op game means your friends either wait or move on without you.

### What Each Player Brings to the Table

Roles in Invokyr are less about fixed classes and more about what you decide to do with limited resources. Still, groups naturally settle into patterns.

| Playstyle | Core job | Best suited for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scout | Push deep into unexplored rooms to find dice | Players with strong map memory |
| Anchor | Stay near the board, hold items, call the rolls | Players who like coordinating the team |
| Medic | Hold healing dice and rescue downed teammates | Patient, unselfish players |
| Bait | Pull monsters away and kite them through the house | Anyone who enjoys chaos and dark comedy |
| Runner | Rush objectives and test the rules early | Experienced horror players |

Because death is not always the end, an aggressive player who goes down is not automatically out of the session. That softens the sting of risky play while keeping the stakes high.

## The Dice, the Cursed Rules, and the Gamemaster

The Gamemaster is the antagonist and the narrator — a once-beloved toy that now rules the house and speaks with a whimsical, bitter voice. It controls the board, changes the rules, and escalates the danger as you progress toward the ultimate Invokyr board.

Dice are the multiplayer currency. Beyond the standard rolls, special dice offer powers to heal, hide, or hinder the creatures hunting you. Nothing is free: the Steam page is explicit that every special die carries risk. Some of the best horror moments in co-op games come from exactly this — a player gambling a one-use item on a plan that either saves everyone or dooms them.

| Dice category | Typical effect | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard dice | Advance the board and trigger events | Outcomes are unpredictable by design |
| Healing dice | Restore health or bring players back into play | Consumed on use, so timing matters |
| Hiding dice | Break line of sight from hunters | Hiding does not stop the board from advancing |
| Hindering dice | Slow or weaken monsters | Risk is attached to the reward |
| Cursed idols | Alter the rules in your favor — or against you | Curses stack as the run goes on |

At Early Access launch, the game is set to include several themed boards, more than 15 monsters, more than 20 events, and over a dozen different dice and cursed idols, plus a shop, upgrades, and environmental secrets. That is a lot of surface area for a group to learn together — and a lot of room for things to go wrong in entertaining ways.

## Survival Tips for Invokyr Multiplayer Groups

Good communication beats good reflexes in almost every co-op horror game, and Invokyr leans harder on that than most. Here is what to keep in mind before your first session.

| Situation | Recommended move |
| --- | --- |
| You find a special die early | Bank it. Do not burn a healing die on the first scary roll |
| A teammate goes down | Check whether they can return before you sprint into danger |
| You are lost in the labyrinth | Stop moving and call your position instead of wandering |
| The rules suddenly change | Re-read the current rule out loud to the group |
| One player is being chased | Split the team: one kites, one keeps rolling |
| You are close to the end of the board | Spend resources now — hoarding loses runs |

Additional practical advice:

- **Assign a caller.** One player decides which die gets rolled next. Group votes sound fair and waste time.
- **Use in-game chat deliberately.** Short callouts about monster locations beat long narration.
- **Track your one-use items.** With dozens of dice in play across a run, a shared mental list prevents waste.
- **Expect the difficulty curve to bite.** Rewards unlock more formidable challenges, so early wins do not mean an easy ride.
- **Play the demo first.** A demo is available on Steam, which is the cheapest way to find out whether your group clicks with the loop.

Accessibility is also worth noting for mixed-ability groups: adjustable text size, subtitle options, custom volume controls, stereo sound, and a design that is playable without quick-time events. That last point matters for a horror game, because it means the scares come from the board and the monsters rather than reflex tests.

## Early Access Roadmap and What Comes Next

Invokyr launches into Early Access, and the developers at Ludogram have been upfront about why. The game stacks many interacting systems — changing rules, monsters, events, and chaotic dice — and the team wants player feedback to shape the balance. They expect the Early Access period to last roughly one year, with regular updates built around new themes.

| Roadmap item | What is planned |
| --- | --- |
| Early Access launch | Complete multiplayer loop for 1 to 6 players, themed boards, 15+ monsters, 20+ events, a dozen-plus dice and cursed idols, shop, upgrades, secrets |
| During Early Access | Regular updates with new themes, monsters, events, items, and features |
| Version 1.0 | New boards and themes, more monsters, events, dice, cursed rules, items, and upgrades |
| Narrative | Expanded mysteries around Invokyr and the Gamemaster |
| Pricing | Expected to rise gradually as new content ships |

Community input is central to that plan. The developers have said they will follow feedback through Steam discussions, Discord, playtests, surveys, reviews, and direct conversations, and that feedback will guide balancing, difficulty, onboarding, and replayability. Community reports from earlier demos and playtests already shaped the game before launch. As the team put it, the Gamemaster may control the board — but the community helps shape what Invokyr becomes.

One honest caveat: Early Access games are works in progress. If you want a finished, fully balanced horror game, waiting is a reasonable choice. If you want to grow with a game while your feedback actually lands, this is the moment.

## Invokyr Multiplayer FAQ

**How many players does Invokyr support?**
Invokyr multiplayer supports one to six players in online co-op. The Steam description phrases it as playing with up to five friends, which adds up to six total including you. Single-player is supported as well.

**Is there local or split-screen co-op?**
The Steam listing highlights online co-op rather than local play, and in-game chat is listed as an interactive element. For now, plan on online sessions with headsets rather than a shared couch screen.

**What happens if a player dies during a run?**
The game states that death is not always the end, so a downed player is not necessarily finished for the session. Healing dice and group coordination can bring teammates back into the fight, which is why item management matters so much.

**When does Invokyr come out, and is there a demo?**
Invokyr is scheduled for Early Access release on October 8, 2026, and the developers expect roughly a year in Early Access before version 1.0. A demo is available on Steam, so you can test the co-op loop with your group before committing.

**Does the game support solo play?**
Yes. The Early Access launch is described as offering a complete multiplayer gameplay loop for one to six players, so solo runs are part of the design rather than an afterthought.
