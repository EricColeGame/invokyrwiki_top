Invokyr drops you and up to three friends into a cursed board game where every roll can summon something that wants your team dead. A solid Invokyr tutorial matters because the game rarely pauses to explain itself — the board is your rulebook, and the shifting house punishes hesitation.

This Invokyr tutorial walks through the complete first-run loop in order: activating the board, managing limited dice, surviving curses and creatures, recovering golden idols, and completing the final escape command together. If your squad keeps wiping before the first idol ever reaches the board, the problem is usually process, not skill.

## Invokyr Tutorial Basics: The Core Loop

Every run follows the same five-phase rhythm. Learning it early makes later decisions — where to walk, what to carry, when to burn a rare die — much easier to justify out loud.

| Phase | Main Action | Primary Risk | Best Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Place and roll a die on the board | Immediate curse or hazard | Read the result before anyone moves |
| Explore | Search rooms, drawers, and side areas | Getting lost or split up | Mark routes and keep voice contact |
| Recover | Find idols, dice, and tools | Carrying objectives through danger | Clear a return path before picking anything up |
| Survive | Avoid creatures, fire, and traps | Health loss or forced detours | Use cover, mirrors, and distractions |
| Escape | Return to the board | Missing a final requirement | Verify every objective before triggering the ending |

The loop is rarely linear in practice. A single bad roll can push you from "Explore" straight into "Survive," and a curse can send you back to "Start" logic even when you are three rooms deep. Treat the table above as a checklist you mentally re-run after every major event.

Player experience from early first-run footage suggests one habit pays off more than any other: narrate what you are doing. A team that says "I'm opening the drawer on the left" gives everyone else a chance to react before a threat triggers.

## Set Up the Board and Manage Your Dice

The board is the anchor of the entire run, not a starting-room decoration. You place dice on it to advance the scenario, and each roll can alter the environment, introduce a curse, reveal a clue, summon a creature, or change your next objective.

Dice double as the game's resource economy. Hidden dice can be recovered throughout the house, and the available game description indicates each one can be used only once. That creates a real timing problem: spending a powerful effect early often makes the final section harder.

| Die or Effect Type | Likely Function | Best Timing | Common Mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard roll | Advances the board sequence | Early progression | Rolling without reading the result |
| Healing effect | Restores survival potential | After meaningful damage | Spending it before a known hazard section |
| Revive effect | Returns a downed player | After a confirmed team loss | Carrying it with no recovery plan |
| Curse effect | Adds a restriction or hazard | Never deliberately sought | Ignoring the exact wording |
| Utility effect | Hides, distracts, or disrupts danger | During an active hunt | Burning it while the house is calm |

### Five Steps for Every Roll

1. **Place the die.** Put the selected die on the board and confirm the game registered it. If nothing triggers, reposition it rather than walking away from the board.
2. **Read the result.** Listen for the clue and inspect the visible instruction. Effects often reference a target, a score threshold, a color, a direction, or a special action.
3. **Assign roles.** Have one player interpret the clue, one track the route, and the rest watch for enemies or items.
4. **Resolve before rushing.** Finish the immediate requirement before wandering off. A clue may demand reaching a score, inserting an item, avoiding a color, or surviving a timer.
5. **Record the outcome.** Track which dice are spent, which rooms are safe, and where the board is. That information becomes critical after a curse rewrites the route.

Limited-use resources are the most common source of regret in this game. Save healing, revival, and defensive effects for moments that block progress or threaten a full team wipe. The same Invokyr tutorial logic applies to any item you cannot replace: if using it does not preserve the run, it is probably too early.

## Surviving Curses, Creatures, and the Shifting House

Danger in Invokyr is not limited to direct pursuit. Curses can strip tools, change navigation rules, or add new conditions for progress. Community reports from early footage describe hazards involving fire, darkness, hostile creatures, color-based rules, sudden attacks, and movement challenges.

The house itself behaves like a labyrinth. Losing the route back to the board is a serious threat, because an objective is only worth anything if your team can physically return with it. Sprinting away from one enemy can create a bigger problem if it dumps you in an unfamiliar wing.

| Threat Pattern | Warning Sign | Survival Priority | Team Callout |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fire hazard | Burning floor or blocked route | Find an alternate path before crossing | "Fire ahead" plus a safe side |
| Darkness curse | Flashlight loss or reduced visibility | Stay together and use known landmarks | "Lights out" plus a regroup point |
| Color rule | Clue referencing black, white, or another color | Identify the safe target before acting | "Safe color is" plus location |
| Movement challenge | Narrow gaps, jumps, or unstable paths | Slow down and check the landing space | "Jump route" or "no jump" |
| Labyrinth effect | Repeated rooms or uncertain direction | Return to the last confirmed landmark | "Board route" plus a room name |

### Landmarks Beat Shortcuts

Mirrors, staircases, distinctive furniture, and the board-room entrance all help you rebuild a route after a chase. When an enemy appears, do not automatically sprint in a straight line. Check first whether the path contains a drop, a trap, a blocked door, or a second enemy. If one player is being chased, the others should guide them toward a known safe room instead of scattering.

The best escape route is the one your whole team can repeat. A slightly slower path with clear landmarks usually beats an unknown shortcut that only one player remembers.

## Recovering Idols and Completing Objectives

Objectives bridge exploration and escape. Golden idols, hidden dice, and other items must be found inside the house, and carrying one is not the same as completing it — most objectives need to be returned to the board or placed at a specific spot.

| Objective Task | Preparation | Completion Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a golden idol | Search side rooms and unusual corners | Confirm the item is visibly carried |
| Recover a hidden die | Inspect drawers, shelves, and off-path rooms | Announce whether it is spent or reserved |
| Follow a clue | Repeat the wording and identify its target | Verify the required action before leaving |
| Return an item | Choose a route with known landmarks | Place it on or near the board as instructed |
| Prepare the finale | Confirm idols, board, and survival tools | Read the final instruction carefully |

### Run Preparation Checklist

- Identify the board room and one reliable return landmark
- Assign a carrier, a navigator, and a rear guard
- Record every discovered die and its likely purpose
- Confirm each idol before leaving the room it was found in
- Reserve at least one recovery effect for a critical failure

A reliable technique is the two-pass route. On the first pass, scout a corridor without committing the entire team. On the second pass, move the objective through it once the main hazard has been identified. This reduces the chance that your carrier gets trapped somewhere with no exit.

## Reaching the Board and Escaping the House

The finale begins once the team has recovered the required objectives and is ready to head back. Do not assume that stepping into the starting room ends the run. The available sequence includes a final challenge, a return command, and an instruction to pick up the game board before escaping.

| Finale Stage | Team Action | Failure Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm objectives | Check idols, board, and required tools | Leaving one item behind |
| Regroup | Bring everyone near the return route | Triggering the ending while someone is lost |
| Read the final clue | Identify the exact interaction or phrase | Guessing from memory |
| Activate escape | Perform the board or spoken command | Triggering the wrong state |
| Verify completion | Wait for the scene or door transition | Moving away too early |

Death is not always permanent. The game description presents dying as reversible in some situations, and revival effects exist for exactly that reason. Still, treat revival as a recovery option rather than a license to take unnecessary risks. One player's careless push can cost the team its only revive.

Before triggering the finale, say the objective list out loud: idols recovered, board ready, team regrouped, final command understood. If you finish the ending and see a new area or an unopened door, keep checking the board and objective state — surviving one section may unlock additional maps or harder scenarios.

## Invokyr Tutorial FAQ

**What is the main goal in Invokyr?**

Play the cursed board game, survive the horrors it summons, recover objectives such as golden idols, return to the board, and escape the house. This Invokyr tutorial treats the board as both the starting point and the finish line.

**How many players can join an Invokyr co-op run?**

The official media kit lists online co-op for up to four players. Solo play is possible, but coordination-heavy mechanics like idol carrying and revive management are noticeably easier with a full squad on voice chat.

**Should I spend special dice as soon as I find them?**

Usually not. Special dice heal, revive, hide, or disrupt threats, and each die can only be used once according to the available description. Reserve them for blocked routes, active hunts, or critical recovery moments.

**How do I finish the first escape sequence?**

Recover the required items, return to the board, pick up the board when instructed, and follow the final spoken or interaction command exactly. The first-run sequence uses the game's name as the ending command.

Invokyr is listed as coming soon, so treat specific enemy behavior, map layouts, and balance details as subject to change during development and future updates. The process, however, should stay stable: identify the rule, mark the route, protect the carrier, and spend resources only when they preserve progress. Follow that loop and your team will convert clues into routes, routes into idols, and idols into a clean escape.
