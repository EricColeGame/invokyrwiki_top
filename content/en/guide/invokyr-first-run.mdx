## Before You Press Play: Setting Up for a Smooth First Run

Most horror co-op groups lose their first session to logistics rather than monsters. Someone has no microphone, someone else misses the subtitles, and nobody agrees on who carries the healing dice. Ten minutes of setup fixes all of that.

### Pick a party size you can actually talk to

The game supports up to six players in a full multiplayer loop, and solo play works too. For a first run, though, a smaller group is easier to coordinate. Three or four players keeps voice chat readable while still leaving room for the "leave someone behind" tension the game is built around.

### Agree on roles before the first roll

Dice in Invokyr are hidden around the house, and each one can only be used once. That scarcity is the whole point. Decide up front who grabs what: one player for defensive or healing dice, one for concealment, one to handle the board. When a monster is already chasing you is not the time to negotiate.

### Set accessibility and audio options early

Invokyr includes adjustable text size, subtitle options, custom volume controls, stereo sound, and a mode that avoids quick-time events. Audio cues matter enormously in a first-person horror game, so drop the music a notch and keep effects and voice chat high.

| Setting | Why it matters | First-run recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Party size | Larger groups are louder and harder to coordinate | 3–4 players for run one |
| Voice chat | Callouts are your only real monster tracker | Push-to-talk or open mic, agreed in advance |
| Subtitles and text size | The Gamemaster's lines carry rules and story beats | Subtitles on, text size bumped up |
| Volume mix | Footsteps and ambient audio signal danger | Music lower, effects and voice higher |
| QTE-free play | Some players freeze under timed prompts | Turn it on if anyone in the group prefers it |

## How a Turn Actually Works: Rules, Dice, and the Gamemaster

The core loop is simple to describe and brutal to survive. You roll at the board, something gets summoned, you explore the house for tools, and you push toward the end so you can escape. What changes is everything else: monsters, events, cursed rules, and the dice you happen to find.

- **Roll the dice at the board** to trigger the next event, trap, or creature.
- **Survive whatever the roll summons** — the Gamemaster does not roll gently.
- **Scour the house** for hidden dice and cursed idols.
- **Spend your finds wisely**, because every die is single-use.
- **Reach the end of the board** and you might win your freedom. Might.

| Dice or item type | What it does | First-run priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healing dice | Restore health to you or an ally | High — save one for an emergency |
| Concealment dice | Help you hide or break line of sight | High in open, exposed biomes |
| Hindering dice | Slow or disrupt whatever is hunting you | Medium — best used to save a teammate |
| Standard dice | Keep the board and its rules moving | Low — spend freely |
| Cursed idols | High-risk, high-reward modifiers | Avoid until you understand the rules |

Player experience with this genre suggests the same pattern every time: groups that treat special dice as emergency buttons survive longer than groups that hoard them.

## Biomes and the House: Don't Get Lost

The house is a labyrinth of rooms, and the developers are blunt about the consequence — lose your way and you may never make it back to the board. Layered on top of that maze are distinct themed biomes, including a jungle, an ice mountain, and an oversized toy chest.

That variety is great for atmosphere and terrible for your sense of direction. Pick a rally point, call out turns as you make them, and never assume you can retrace your steps under pressure.

| Biome | Theme | First-run approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jungle | Overgrown, dense scenery | Move in pairs; the path back is easy to lose |
| Ice Mountain | Cold, stark environment | Keep audio high — sound travels further than sight |
| Oversized Toy Chest | Warped-scale, surreal space | Re-check landmarks; scale tricks your memory |
| The board room | Your anchor to the game itself | Return often, even if you have not found a die |

## A Step-by-Step First-Run Walkthrough

Here is a practical order of operations for your opening session.

1. **Let the intro play.** The Gamemaster's opening narration sets the tone and hints at the rules you are about to face.
2. **Confirm comms and roles.** Thirty seconds of "who has what" saves ten minutes of panic later.
3. **Take the first roll together.** Stay near the board for the first summon so the whole group sees how a monster behaves.
4. **Explore in pairs, not alone.** One pair scouts, one pair holds the board area.
5. **Bank your first special die.** If you find a healing or concealment die, agree on who holds it.
6. **Use the shop and upgrades between boards.** Progression carries across boards, so small purchases compound.
7. **Push for the ultimate Invokyr Board** only when the group still has resources left to spend.

| Phase | Goal | Time pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening rolls | Learn the board and the first monster | Low |
| Early exploration | Find dice and cursed idols | Medium |
| Mid-run | Survive combined rules and events | High |
| Final push | Reach the end of the board and escape | Very high |

## Common First-Run Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most Invokyr first run failures come down to a handful of repeatable errors rather than bad luck. Fix these and your second attempt will feel like a different game.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Splitting up immediately | Lost players cannot reach the board in time | Move in pairs with a rally point |
| Hoarding special dice | Dice are single-use and do not carry over | Spend them the moment they save a life |
| Ignoring the Gamemaster | Rules change, and the narration telegraphs them | Turn subtitles on and listen closely |
| Playing in silence | No callouts means no monster tracking | Narrate what you see, even when it is boring |
| Skipping the demo | You learn the loop on launch day instead of now | Download the free demo and run a practice session |
| Over-extending the final push | You reach the end with nothing left | Retreat, restock, then commit |

## FAQ

**What should I do before my Invokyr first run?**
Download the free demo, set your subtitles and volume mix, and agree on party size and roles with your group. Ten minutes of preparation prevents most of the confusion that ruins an opening session.

**Is Invokyr playable solo?**
Yes. The game supports single-player as well as online co-op for up to six players. Solo runs are quieter and more tense, but the full gameplay loop works without a group.

**How many players is best for a first run?**
Three or four is the sweet spot. You get enough hands for dice management and exploration without the voice chat turning into a crowd.

**What happens if everyone dies on the first run?**
Death is not always the end in Invokyr, and rewards carry forward even when a run goes badly. Treat the first attempt as a tutorial, then spend your progress on upgrades and try again.
