Few co-op horror games put a literal board game at the center of the nightmare, and that is exactly why this **Invokyr review** deserves your attention. Invokyr is a first-person online co-op survival horror game from developer Ludogram, built around a cursed 1970s board game that summons monsters, traps, and shifting rules every time somebody rolls. ## What Is Invokyr? Setting, Story, and the Gamemaster

The premise is simple and genuinely unsettling. You are trapped in a cold, dark, abandoned house. On the table sits a dusty board game humming with energy, and a voice — whimsical but bitter — invites you to play. That voice belongs to the Gamemaster, once a beloved toy and now the ruler of the house. Escape is possible, but only by finishing the game.

From there, Invokyr becomes a loop of rolling, surviving, and improvising. Every roll summons something new: a trap, a curse, a monster, or a rule change that rewrites how the round works. The developers describe it as a psychological horror experience where the board itself is the antagonist, and the unpredictable interaction between dice, events, and monsters is the whole point.

## How to Play Invokyr: Dice, Curses, and the Core Loop

The core loop is exploration plus gambling. You scour the labyrinthine house to recover dice hidden inside it. Each die can only be used once, so every roll is a resource decision, not a reflex. Choose wisely, because the house does not hand out second chances cheaply.

| Dice Category | Effect | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healing dice | Restore health to you or a teammate | Uses your single charge on recovery instead of escape |
| Concealment dice | Help you hide from whatever is hunting you | Hiding stalls progress while the board keeps ticking |
| Hindering dice | Slow, distract, or obstruct a pursuer | Risky — the game warns nothing comes without a cost |
| Cursed idols | Additional modifiers found while exploring | Curses stack and can make later rounds harder |

Beyond dice, Invokyr layers in traps, curses, and rule changes that trigger on turns. The developers emphasize that so many systems interact with each other that no two sessions unfold the same way, which is both the appeal and the reason Early Access feedback is so valuable to them.

Combined with the shifting rule system, that is what separates Invokyr from the standard "run away from the ghost" formula.

## Multiplayer Guide: Surviving With 1 to 6 Players

Invokyr supports single-player and online co-op for up to six total players. That range changes the game dramatically, and the developers lean into it: you can work together, or you can leave someone behind. Death is not always the end, which opens the door to risky rescue plays and some genuinely funny dark-comedy moments.

| Group Size | What It Feels Like | Practical Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo | Slow, tense, resource-starved | Prioritize concealment dice and avoid unnecessary rolls |
| 2–3 players | Tight, coordinated, high tension | Split only when a die location is confirmed |
| 4–6 players | Chaotic, loud, comedic | Assign a caller who tracks active rules out loud |

A few practical habits help new groups survive longer:

- **Track the active rules out loud.** Invokyr's rules change mid-run, and the most common cause of a wipe is a player acting on a rule that expired two turns ago.
- **Bank your dice.** Because each die is single-use, holding one for an emergency is often stronger than spending it immediately.
- **Learn the biomes.** The jungle, ice mountain, and toy chest layouts reward different movement and hiding strategies.
- **Communicate about revives.** "Death is not always the end" means a downed teammate may still be recoverable — decide fast whether the rescue is worth the risk.

The accessibility options are also worth noting for groups with mixed experience levels.
