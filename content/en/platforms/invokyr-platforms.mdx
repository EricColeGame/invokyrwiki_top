## Multiplayer Across Invokyr Platforms: How Co-op Actually Works

The multiplayer design is where Invokyr separates itself from a crowded field of co-op horror games. Instead of a fixed map and predictable scares, the game layers a board game on top of the exploration: you roll dice, the Gamemaster answers with monsters and events, and the rules themselves mutate as you progress.

Steam's short summary invites you to "play with up to 5 friends," while the longer description says online co-op supports up to 6 players. Those two lines describe the same lobby — five friends plus you — so plan for a six-person party maximum.

| Mode | Player Count | How It Plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo | 1 | Full loop with no one to revive you |
| Small co-op | 2–3 | Tighter, easier to coordinate |
| Full co-op | 4–6 | Chaos, split objectives, and friendly betrayal |

Community reports from the playtests suggest that larger lobbies create the most memorable moments — and the most disasters. Death is not necessarily permanent, which softens the sting of a bad roll, but splitting up to hunt for dice is how teams get picked off one at a time. Special dice can heal, hide, or hinder, and each one is single-use, so deciding who carries what is a genuine strategic decision rather than flavor text.

## FAQ

**What platforms is Invokyr on?**

At launch, Invokyr platforms are limited to PC via Steam, where it enters Early Access on October 8, 2026. No console, mobile, Mac, Linux, or VR versions have been announced. If you want to play on release day, a Windows PC with Steam is your only confirmed option.

**Can I play Invokyr on Steam Deck?**

Not confirmed. Steam currently displays an "Unknown" compatibility rating for the game, meaning Valve has not verified how well it runs on the handheld. Wishlisting the game will notify you if that status changes.

**How many players can join a co-op session?**

Online co-op supports up to six players total. The store page also phrases it as playing with up to five friends, which describes the same lobby — you plus five others. Single-player is fully supported as well.

**Should I buy during Early Access or wait?**

It depends on your tolerance for an unfinished game. Early Access launches with several themed boards, more than 15 monsters, over 20 events, and a full multiplayer loop, and the price is expected to rise as content is added. If you want a polished, complete experience, waiting for the 1.0 release is the safer bet.
