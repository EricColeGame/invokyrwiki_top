## How to Estimate Whether Your PC Can Handle Invokyr

Until the official **invokyr system requirements** arrive, you can make a reasonable guess using genre baselines. Co-op horror games built in this style — first-person, dark environments, dynamic monster AI, four-plus players networked together — tend to land in a predictable hardware band.

The table below is **general guidance for this genre, not an official spec sheet**. Treat it as a sanity check, not gospel.

| Hardware Tier | Typical Genre Baseline | What It Means for Invokyr |

| Entry-level | Quad-core CPU, 8 GB RAM, GTX 1050 Ti / RX 570 class GPU | Likely playable at 1080p low, but expect dips during multi-monster chaos |

### Three Things That Will Actually Matter

1. **RAM headroom.** Online co-op games load more than single-player ones — networked player states, synced monster AI, and shared physics. 16 GB is the safe floor for a six-player session.
2. **CPU single-core speed.** Horror AI, line-of-sight checks, and dice-rule calculations are often CPU-bound. A fast 6-core beats a slow 12-core here.
3. **An SSD.** Labyrinthine houses with streaming rooms punish mechanical drives. Install on an SSD if you have one.

### Don't Forget the Network Side

"Can I run it?" is only half the question — Invokyr is a multiplayer-first experience. Steam's content descriptors note in-game chat and online interactivity, so a stable connection matters as much as a capable GPU. Wired ethernet or solid 5 GHz Wi-Fi will do more for your session quality than one extra GPU tier.
