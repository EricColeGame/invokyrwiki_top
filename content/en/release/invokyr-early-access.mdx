## What Kind of Game Is Invokyr?

Invokyr is a first-person co-op horror game built around a haunted board game. You and your friends are trapped in a groaning, abandoned house where a dusty 1970s board sits on the table, humming with energy. A whimsical but bitter voice — the Gamemaster — invites you to play. Escape is possible, but only on the board's terms.

The core loop is a mix of board game turns and survival horror exploration. Every roll summons something: a monster, a trap, a curse, or a rules change that reshapes the run. You move between the board and the house itself, scavenging for special dice hidden in the rooms.

A few design details stand out:

- **Dice are consumable.** Each special die can only be used once, so choosing when to spend one is a real decision rather than a formality.
- **Dice powers cut both ways.** Some heal, hide, or hinder hunters, but nothing comes without risk.
- **The house is a labyrinth.** Get lost in the wrong biome and you may never make it back to the board.
- **Death is not always the end.** The co-op design leaves room for players to be dragged back in, which keeps sessions tense rather than punishing.
- **Themes shift the environment.** Confirmed biomes include a jungle, an ice mountain, and an oversized toy chest.

The blend of dark comedy and psychological horror is deliberate. The tone sits somewhere between dread and absurdity, which is part of what makes the board-game framing work.

## Tips for Your First Co-Op Session

Early access horror games reward players who treat the first few runs as reconnaissance. A few practical habits will make the launch week smoother:

1. **Pick roles before you roll.** With up to six players, splitting into a board crew and a scavenging crew prevents the whole team from clustering in one room.
2. **Bank your special dice for emergencies.** Since each die is single-use, spending one on a minor inconvenience early usually backfires later.
3. **Learn monster behavior before you fight it.** The developers emphasize that each creature hunts differently, so observation beats brute force.
4. **Map the house mentally.** The labyrinth design punishes wandering, and different biomes change how the space reads.
5. **Buy upgrades deliberately.** Between-board progression and the shop mean resources compound, so a bad purchase echoes for several runs.
6. **Check accessibility settings first.** Turning on subtitles and tuning volume before the first jump scare makes the audio cues easier to read.

Community reports from the demo and playtest period consistently point to communication as the deciding factor in whether a run succeeds. The Gamemaster may control the board, but the players control the chaos.
