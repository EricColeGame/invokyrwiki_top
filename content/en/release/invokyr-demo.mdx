## What Is the Invokyr Demo?

The Invokyr demo is a free Steam download from Ludogram, the studio handling both development and publishing. It launched on June 2, 2026, and it's built as a genuine slice of the full release rather than a stripped-down teaser — you get the complete cursed-board-game loop, online co-op, and a house full of things that would rather you never reached the final square.

The premise is easy to explain and hard to shake. You and your friends are trapped in an abandoned house with a dusty board game sitting on the table. A voice — whimsical on the surface, bitter underneath — invites you to play. That voice belongs to the Gamemaster, once a beloved toy and now the ruler of the house. Escape is possible, but only on its terms.

## How the Cursed Board Game Actually Plays

The core loop is a gamble wrapped in a scavenger hunt. You roll the dice, and something answers. Sometimes it's a curse, sometimes a trap, sometimes a creature that has decided you look interesting. Your job is to survive the fallout, push toward the end of the board, and try not to lose your nerve in the dark.

Dice aren't just a random-number generator here — they're a resource you have to physically recover. Special dice are hidden throughout the house, and each one can only be used a single time, which turns every pickup into a group decision. Do you burn a healing die now, or save it for the turn where someone is bleeding out in a hallway?

| System | How it works | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Dice rolls | Each roll triggers an event, hazard, or summon | Keeps every turn unpredictable |
| Hidden dice | Found by exploring the house, single use only | Creates scarcity and forces teamwork |
| Special dice | Grant effects like healing, hiding, or hindering hunters | High reward, real risk attached |
| The Gamemaster | Narrates, taunts, and controls the pace of the game | Serves as both antagonist and game master |
| Creatures | Each monster type has its own hunting behavior | You can't learn one pattern and coast |
| Traps and curses | Triggered across turns and rooms | Punishes players who rush blindly |

### The House and Its Biomes

The house isn't a single haunted corridor — it's a labyrinth of rooms, and getting lost is a genuine failure state. Wander too far off the path and you may never find your way back to the board at all. The environments shift into distinct biomes, including a jungle, an ice mountain, and an oversized toy chest, which gives the demo a surprising amount of visual variety for a free release.

Community reports suggest the toy chest biome is the one that catches new players off guard most often, partly because the scale makes everything feel wrong in exactly the way the game wants.

Invokyr was designed around playing with other people, but the demo doesn't force you into a group. According to the store listing, it supports both single-player and online co-op for up to four players total, with in-game chat included as an interactive element.

The tension comes from how the game treats cooperation. Working together is clearly the smart play, but the design leaves room for selfish decisions — and death isn't always the final word, which means a downed teammate can still shape how a run ends.

### Tips for Getting More Out of Your First Runs

- **Communicate constantly.** Call out what you rolled and where you're heading. Silence gets people killed.
- **Don't hoard dice.** A special die you never use is worth nothing. Spend it when a teammate is in trouble.
- **Learn each creature's tell.** Monsters hunt differently, so watching behavior matters more than memorizing routes.
- **Anchor yourself to the board.** When you explore, note landmarks so you can find your way back before the house swallows you.
- **Split up carefully.** Spreading out finds dice faster, but a lone player is an easy target.
- **Replay the demo.** The reward structure encourages another run, and the unpredictability means no two sessions play the same.
