| Setting area | What it affects | Why it matters |

A sensible order of operations is display first, then audio, then input. ## Controls and Keybinds: What Invokyr Currently Offers

Controls are the part of Invokyr settings that generates the most discussion, and for good reason. Movement layout is personal. Plenty of players grew up on WASD, but a significant group prefers ESDF, IJKL, or a left-handed arrangement — and those players feel the absence of remapping immediately.

### What Players Are Reporting About Custom Keybinds

In a Steam community thread, a player explained that they were interested in the game because they liked its themes and gameplay, but noticed while pre-configuring their setup that the demo offered no way to change keybindings. They asked whether custom keybinds were planned for a later version or the full release, and specifically mentioned that they do not prefer WASD layouts.

No developer response appears in that thread as it was captured, so the honest answer is that the request is on the community's radar but not confirmed either way. Treat any claim about a future keybind menu as unverified until it comes from the developer directly. What you can do right now is work around the limitation, and the workarounds are genuinely good.

### Workarounds You Can Use Today

Because the restriction is at the game level, you can solve most of it one layer up — in your operating system, your peripherals, or your platform's input layer.

| Workaround | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Input remapping | Rebind keys and buttons through the Steam client before the game sees them | Steam Deck, controllers, and desktop players who launch through Steam |
| Keyboard remapping software | Swap physical key output at the OS level | Players who prefer ESDF, IJKL, or left-handed layouts |
| Peripheral software | Assign side buttons and macros on your mouse or keyboard | Players who want extra actions within thumb reach |
| Community layouts | Download or build a shared Steam Input profile | Quick starts without configuring everything by hand |

Steam Input is the strongest option for most people. It sits between your hardware and the game, so the game keeps receiving the inputs it expects while your hands do something different. You can also build layers — a base layout for exploration and a shifted layout for combat — which is often better than a straight remap.

One caveat: remapping at the OS or client level means your on-screen prompts may no longer match your actual keys. If the game displays a "press W" hint and you're pressing E, expect a brief adjustment period. It's a small cost for a much more comfortable setup.

## Display, Audio, and Performance Settings Worth Adjusting

Display options rarely get the attention they deserve, but they shape everything else. A resolution that's slightly too high for your hardware produces frame drops that feel like input lag, which players often misattribute to the controls. Start conservative, confirm the game feels responsive, then push quality upward until you find the edge.

| Setting | What it does | Recommended starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | Number of pixels rendered | Match your monitor's native resolution, then scale down if performance suffers |
| Window mode | Fullscreen, borderless, windowed | Fullscreen for performance, borderless for fast alt-tabbing |
| V-Sync | Syncs frames to your refresh rate | Off if you notice input delay; on if screen tearing bothers you |
| Frame rate cap | Limits rendered frames | Cap slightly below your monitor's refresh rate for steadier pacing |
| Effects and shadows | Visual fidelity of lighting and particles | Lower first if you need performance back |
| Camera behavior | Shake, sway, and motion intensity | Reduce if long sessions leave you uncomfortable |

Audio is the setting most players skip, and it's a mistake. In action games, sound effects carry information that visuals can't always deliver when the screen is busy. If music is drowning out effects, pull the music channel down rather than the master volume — you keep the cues without the fatigue.

Comfort settings deserve a mention too. Community threads in the game's hub have touched on motion sickness, which is a common issue in fast, effect-heavy games. If you feel off after twenty minutes, try a smaller field of view change, a steadier frame rate, and a break from full-screen camera motion before you assume the game isn't for you.

## Controller and Steam Deck Settings for Invokyr

Handheld play is a real consideration here, and the community reports are mixed but useful. One player noted that the game is playable on Steam Deck, but that some functionality is lost — they specifically mentioned being unable to crouch, and found certain interactions unintuitive. That's the kind of detail worth knowing before you commit to a handheld run.

| Input method | Strengths | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Keyboard and mouse | Precision aiming, full key access | Fixed layout unless you remap externally |
| Standard controller | Comfortable for long sessions, familiar sticks | Some actions may be harder to reach without remapping |
| Steam Deck (built-in controls) | Portable, suspend-friendly | Community reports of missing functions like crouch |
| Steam Deck with custom layout | Recovers missing actions via Steam Input | Requires setup time and testing |

If you're playing on Deck, the fix for a missing action is usually to bind it manually through Steam Input rather than waiting for a patch. Map the unavailable function to a back button or a touchpad press, then test it in a safe area before you rely on it in a fight. Expect to spend ten or fifteen minutes tuning, and expect the result to feel better than the default.

## Troubleshooting Common Invokyr Settings Problems

Most settings complaints come down to a handful of causes. Work through this table before you assume something is broken.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Keys don't match on-screen prompts | External remapping or a custom layout | Rebuild prompts mentally, or note the mapping on paper |
| Actions feel delayed | Low frame rate rather than input lag | Lower effects and shadows, cap the frame rate |
| Settings reset after restarting | Profile or cloud sync conflict | Verify the game's config files, check cloud sync status |
| No crouch or missing action on Deck | Input mapping gap reported by players | Bind the action manually through Steam Input |
| Motion discomfort | Camera shake, unstable frame rate, wide FOV | Reduce camera motion, stabilize performance, take breaks |
| Audio cues hard to hear | Music too loud relative to effects | Lower music, raise effects, avoid raising master |

A useful habit: change one thing at a time. Adjusting display, audio, and input simultaneously makes it impossible to know which change helped. One variable per session keeps the process short.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Invokyr Settings

**Does Invokyr have custom keybinds?**
In the demo build that players have tested, community reports indicate there is no in-game keybind customization. A player raised the question directly in the Steam discussions and asked whether it would arrive in a later version, but no developer confirmation was visible in that thread. Until that changes, external remapping is the practical route.

**Can I play Invokyr on Steam Deck?**
Yes, according to player experience. One community member reported that the game runs on Deck but that some functionality is lost, including crouch, and that certain interactions felt unintuitive. A custom Steam Input layout usually recovers those missing actions.

**What should I change first in Invokyr settings?**
Display options, then audio, then controls. Display determines whether the game feels responsive at all, audio determines whether you can hear combat cues, and controls are easiest to judge once performance is stable.

**Why don't my remapped keys match the game's prompts?**
Because the game still thinks it's receiving its default inputs. Remapping at the OS or Steam Input level changes what your hardware sends, not what the game displays. It's a cosmetic mismatch rather than a functional problem, and most players stop noticing within a session or two.

The bottom line: Invokyr settings are limited in the current build, but the limitations are workable. Tune display first, protect your audio cues, and use Steam Input to shape the controls around your hands rather than the other way around. If custom keybinds do arrive in a future version, everything you set up now will still be useful — and if they don't, you'll already be playing comfortably.
