## What Invokyr Dice Are and Why They Matter

Every round of Invokyr begins with a roll, and every roll can rewrite the entire night. In this co-op survival horror game from developer Ludogram, invokyr dice are far more than a random number generator — they are the currency you spend to heal a fallen friend, hide from a hunter, or buy one more turn at the table. If you don't understand how invokyr dice work before you sit down with your squad, the Gamemaster will happily teach you the hard way.

The setup is simple and nasty. You and up to five friends are trapped in a cold, dark house with a dusty 1970s board game that hums with energy. A voice — whimsical, bitter, and clearly enjoying itself — invites you to play. Roll the dice, survive whatever the board summons, and reach the end to earn your freedom. Maybe.

Because the board controls the pacing and the dice control your options, the two systems are inseparable. The board decides what appears in the house. Your dice decide whether you can actually answer it. That's why experienced players treat every found die as a resource to be budgeted, not a button to mash the moment things get scary.

| Layer of Dice | Where It Comes From | Who Controls It | What It Influences |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board rolls | The cursed board's turn structure | Active player or team | Monster spawns, events, traps, cursed rules |
| Found dice | Hidden inside rooms during exploration | Whoever picks it up | Healing, hiding, hindering hunters |
| Cursed idols | Discovered while scavenging | The carrier | Rule twists and risk-reward modifiers |

## How Invokyr Dice Fit Into a Normal Turn

A typical round in Invokyr cycles through four loose phases, and dice touch three of them. Understanding that rhythm matters more than memorizing any single effect, because the worst mistake new teams make is spending a die during the wrong phase.

| Phase | What Happens | Dice Involvement |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Board Roll | The team rolls and the board reacts | Direct — this roll summons the threat |
| 2. Scavenge | Players spread out through the house | Indirect — you're hunting for hidden dice |
| 3. Survive | Hunters, traps, and curses activate | Direct — special dice save lives here |
| 4. Spend | Recovered dice get used | Direct — one use, then gone forever |

The scavenge phase is where most teams fall apart. The house is a labyrinth of rooms, and if you lose your way you may never make it back to the board. That creates a genuine tension: the dice you need are usually tucked away in the parts of the map that are hardest to navigate, and wandering too far from the board can cost you the run before a single monster shows up.

Community reports from the demo and playtest period suggest that new groups consistently under-explore early and over-explore late. The fix is to treat the first few rounds as a shopping trip — grab dice while the board is relatively calm, then stop wandering once the curse stack starts climbing.

## Special Dice: Heal, Hide, or Hinder

Ludogram has confirmed that some dice grant powers to heal, hide, or hinder the things hunting you, and that nothing comes without a cost. The developer has also stated that Early Access will launch with more than a dozen different dice and cursed idols, with additional dice planned for the road to 1.0.

Exact die names, numerical values, and drop rates are still being finalized, so treat the framework below as a way to think about the system rather than a complete list.

| Effect Family | What It Typically Does | Best Moment to Use It | The Hidden Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing | Restores a downed or injured teammate | Right after a chase breaks off | You may have just spent your only recovery |
| Hiding | Breaks line of sight or conceals a player | When a hunter has your trail | The threat still exists; it just lost you |
| Hindering | Slows, redirects, or blocks a hunter | To buy time for a revive or an objective | Aggression can escalate the curse |
| Utility | Manipulates the board, rules, or movement | When the board stalls out | Outcomes are the least predictable |

A useful mental model: healing dice solve problems that already happened, hiding dice solve problems happening right now, and hindering dice solve problems that are about to happen. If your team can agree on that vocabulary before the first roll, you'll waste far fewer dice on panic decisions.

## The One-Use Rule and Dice Economy

This is the single most important mechanical fact about invokyr dice: each die recovered from the house can only be used once. There is no recharging, no cooldown, and no second activation. Every die is a one-shot consumable, which turns the game into a resource management puzzle wearing a horror mask.

That scarcity is deliberate. It's also why the "should I use it now?" question creates more arguments between friends than any monster in the game.

| Situation | Spend a Die? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hunter is between you and the board | Yes | Positioning beats hoarding every time |
| Teammate is down with a hunter nearby | Yes | Death isn't always the end, but revives cost more |
| Board roll is coming and the curse stack is low | Usually no | Save it for the phase where it matters |
| You're lost and separated from the group | Depends | A utility die may be worth it; a heal probably isn't |
| Final stretch toward the ultimate board | Yes | There's no value in an unused die at the finish line |

Three economy rules of thumb that hold up across most runs:

- **Never spend two dice to solve one problem.** Doubling up on a single threat is the fastest way to run dry before the board's hardest stretch.
- **Assign a die owner, not a die.** One player should track who is carrying what so nobody has to ask mid-chase.
- **Count dice, not kills.** The team that survives is usually the team that knows exactly how many one-use dice remain.

## Risk, Curses, and the Escalation Ladder

Every roll in Invokyr summons something, and the board gets more dangerous as the team progresses toward the final board. Cursed rules stack on top of each other, which means a die that feels like a safe panic button in round two can be a genuine gamble in round twelve.

| Risk Raiser | Risk Reducer |
|---|---|
| Stacked cursed rules | Clearing an objective before rolling again |
| Splitting the party across biomes | Staying within shouting distance of the board |
| Using hindering dice aggressively | Using hiding dice to reset a chase |
| Ignoring environmental secrets | Exploring early when the board is calm |

The house itself adds another variable. It contains distinct biomes — jungle, ice mountain, and an oversized toy chest among them — and each one changes how easy it is to navigate, hide, or get cornered. A hiding die that works beautifully in a dense jungle room may be nearly useless in an open toy chest.

## Co-op Strategy: Splitting Invokyr Dice Across the Team

With one to six players supported, the ideal composition changes every run, but the underlying logic doesn't. Dice should sit with the players who are most likely to need them and least likely to die holding them.

| Role | Dice Priority | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Scout / Explorer | Utility and hiding dice | They're alone most often and furthest from help |
| Anchor / Board Watcher | Healing dice | They're the one who can reach a downed player fastest |
| Runner / Objective Pusher | Hindering dice | They create the windows everyone else uses |
| Support / Flex | Whatever the team lacks | They patch holes as the board escalates |

Two habits separate strong teams from chaotic ones. First, call out dice before you pick them up — a healing die in the hands of a player who is already lost in the labyrinth is a wasted healing die. Second, remember that death is not always the end. Downed players can return, so a die spent on a risky revive isn't automatically a bad trade.

## FAQ

**Can you reuse invokyr dice once you find them?**
No. Every die recovered from the house is a single-use item, so each one is a decision rather than a tool you can rely on repeatedly.

**How many dice are in Invokyr at launch?**
Early Access will include more than a dozen different dice and cursed idols, with additional dice planned as the game moves toward its 1.0 release.

**What's the best time to use a special die?**
Use healing dice after a chase ends, hiding dice when you're actively being tracked, and hindering dice when your team needs a window to revive someone or push an objective.

**Do dice get stronger as the game goes on?**
The dice themselves don't scale, but the risks do. Stacked cursed rules and tougher biomes mean a die that felt safe early can become a real gamble later in the run.

**Is Invokyr better solo or with friends?**
It supports one to six players, and the dice economy is far more forgiving with a coordinated group — solo runs demand much tighter budgeting of every single-use die you find.
